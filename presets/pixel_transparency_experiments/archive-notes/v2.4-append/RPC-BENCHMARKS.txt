All-append RPC measurements (2026-10-16)
=======================================

Retroid Pocket Classic, Adreno A12, Android 14, Vulkan, RetroArch 1.22.2
(16734fe). Both routes use Authentic GBC Fast and full quality defaults: Full
paper texture, seven rainbow colors and Smooth shadows. Sensor parameters
retain their defaults for live-gameplay timing. No existing preset was replaced.

Uncapped whole-application FPS
------------------------------

Scene / viewport                 Plain native  Plain append  Shimmer native  Shimmer append
Mario Tennis, screen fit               78.67         60.44           62.60           46.07
Mario Tennis, integer                  89.02         67.73           70.77           51.50
Pokemon Lazarus, screen fit            90.06         70.27           72.19           53.88
Pokemon Lazarus, integer               95.55         74.29           76.42           57.09

Each cell is the median of three run medians, using frames 128-320 after
loading the same copied save state. Native/append order alternates. Audio,
VSync, frameskip and runahead are off. There are 48 timing runs. Mario Tennis
uses Gambatte slot 9 at its title screen; Pokemon Lazarus uses mGBA slot 0
in the outdoor town scene. Inputs and states match the prior benchmark set.
Viewports: GB fit 1200x1080, GB integer 1120x1008, GBA fit 1240x827, and GBA
integer 1200x800. Exact core options and source hashes are recorded with the
private capture evidence. Device battery temperature ranged from 26 to 28 C.

Normal playback with sound and VSync
------------------------------------

Append Shimmer, one matched scene-window run per case:
  Mario Tennis, screen fit: 45.91 FPS
  Mario Tennis, integer: 51.59 FPS
  Pokemon Lazarus, screen fit: 53.25 FPS
  Pokemon Lazarus, integer: 56.41 FPS
  Mario Tennis screen fit, native-shimmer: 59.92 FPS
  Mario Tennis screen fit, append-pt: 60.08 FPS

These playback checks are single runs, not three-run medians. Plain append
clears 60 FPS uncapped in all four cases, but has little margin at GB screen
fit. Appended Shimmer misses 60 in every tested case.

Why appending costs more
------------------------

The Authentic GBC Fast chains still use five passes for plain transparency
or seven for Shimmer. The append route copies the full LCD output rather than
the small native image: 1200x1080 versus 160x144 for the GB screen-fit case.
Its RGBA32F shadow target is fixed at 1536x512 (12 MiB), compared with the
native cache at 960x288 (4.22 MiB GB) or 1440x320 (7.03 MiB GBA). The generic
compositor also carries a direct-kernel fallback for larger inputs.

Bounded diagnostic tests at Mario Tennis screen fit were single runs:
  small-cache: 48.09 FPS
  no-fallback: 52.90 FPS
  copy16: 48.33 FPS
  copy8: 49.86 FPS
  copy16-small: 50.66 FPS
  copy8-small: 52.23 FPS
  screen-copy32: 49.53 FPS
  screen-copy8: 52.73 FPS

These variants isolate cache allocation, copy precision and shader structure.
The no-fallback variant cannot support arbitrary larger inputs, and an 8-bit
copy cannot preserve HDR grid output. No diagnostic reached 60 FPS. The
committed experiment retains its original full-precision copy, cache and
fallback; diagnostic source files were removed from the checkout.

Image validation and preservation
---------------------------------

Four paired RPC captures are pixel-identical: plain transparency and Shimmer
on native GB/GBA saved-frame images at screen fit. This uses the built-in
imageviewer with PT_ACCEL_ENABLE=0 and all other quality defaults. Each
preset also produces two identical repeated captures. These images are static
renders, not gameplay timing samples. Other LCD combinations were not compared
on the device in this test; the Mac differences remain documented separately.

Normal configuration hashes match before and after testing. Copied save states
and isolated settings keep normal saves and presets separate. The existing
production pipeline remains unchanged. The append candidate is installed in a
separate test bundle under RetroArch/shaders/pixel-transparency-v2.4-append-test-gy0uoij4/.

These are short, scene-specific comparisons, not a thermal soak or a claim
about every game. Physical tilt and other graphics backends remain unverified.
